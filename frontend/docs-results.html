Reading the results

After a fit, the report shows the convergence status, the fitted parameters with uncertainties, and goodness-of-fit statistics. Convergence means the fitting procedure met its stopping conditions; it does not by itself establish that the model describes the experiment adequately.

Parameters and uncertainties

Each parameter is reported with its standard uncertainty from the fit's covariance matrix. Quote a result to a precision consistent with its uncertainty - usually one or two significant figures on the uncertainty, with the value rounded to the same decimal place.

χ², NDF, and χ²/NDF

χ² measures the total squared mismatch between data and model in units of the measurement uncertainties. NDF (degrees of freedom) is the number of fitted points minus the number of free parameters. Their ratio, χ²/NDF, is near 1 when the model fits the data to within the stated error bars. A value much greater than 1 suggests the model is wrong or the uncertainties are underestimated; much less than 1 suggests the uncertainties are overestimated. The p-value is the probability of a χ² this large or larger if the model were correct.

A reduced χ² near 1 is consistent with a good fit but does not prove the model; a poor value is informative but should be read together with the residuals. Reduced χ² alone cannot classify a fit as good or bad.

Residuals and pulls

Optional diagnostic plots appear below the main plot: residuals (data − fit), pulls (residual ÷ uncertainty), the data/fit ratio, the percentage difference, and a histogram of residuals. For a good fit the pulls scatter like a standard normal - roughly two-thirds within ±1 and very few beyond ±3 - with no trend across X. A bow or wave in the residuals is the clearest sign of a wrong model.

Correlations and the confidence band

The parameter correlation matrix shows how strongly the fitted parameters trade off against one another; large correlations mean the data do not determine those parameters separately. An optional pointwise confidence band (68%, 95%, or 99%) shows the uncertainty in the fitted curve from the parameter covariance. It is not a prediction interval for new measurements.

Excluding points

Use Point exclusions… to remove specific measurements from an XY fit without deleting them - for a known bad reading, detector saturation, or a point outside the regime your model describes. Select rows (Shift-click for a range), mark them excluded, and optionally record a reason. Excluded points appear as open grey markers, are omitted from the fit and diagnostics, and their reasons are saved and exported. The original values remain in your data. To restrict a fit to a range instead of individual points, set a fit range under the parameter table.

If a fit fails or looks wrong

Check the parameter order, signs, units, and the function definition first, then try other physically reasonable starting values. If very different guesses reach the same curve and parameters, the result is numerically stable; if they reach different solutions, compare the fit statistic and residuals and consider whether your measured range can distinguish them. Large uncertainties or strong correlations indicate information missing from the data - changing the guesses cannot supply it. Do not adjust measurement uncertainties merely to make a fit look acceptable.
